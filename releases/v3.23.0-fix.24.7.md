## What's New

### Operator-curated proxies never give up on auth

Proxies you pay for and add by hand should not be discarded just because the platform's auth path had a rough stretch. Previously, any proxy that exhausted `maxAuthFailures` went permanently offline until a restart — including file, internal, and `[direct]` proxies.

Now the leash depends on where the proxy came from:

- **File / internal / direct proxies** switch to a **slow capped retry** (5m → 10m → 15m, with jitter) instead of going dark forever. A transient platform issue no longer sidelines a good paid proxy for the rest of the run.
- **URL-sourced proxies** keep the short leash — they give up and are requeued after their backoff window, since a noisy public list shouldn't hold an auth slot.

### URL requeue decoupled from the reload engine

The per-proxy give-up requeue used to be a goroutine that slept 15 minutes, consulted a `proxyGiveUpCooldown` map, wrote a reload trigger, then rechecked 30 seconds later. That machinery (~110 lines: the cooldown map, its mutex, and helper functions) is gone, replaced by a single `time.AfterFunc` per proxy.

`reload()` now acquires `proxy.lock` before proceeding, so hot-reloads serialize cleanly with cross-process operations like `proxy remove-dead` and URL source fetches — no more races between a requeue and a concurrent reload.

## Under the Hood

- **PR #119** — operator-curated proxies switch to slow capped retry on auth exhaustion; URL-sourced proxies retain the `time.AfterFunc` requeue.
- **PR #120** — replaced `scheduleGiveUpRequeue` with `time.AfterFunc`; deleted the `proxyGiveUpCooldown` map/mutex/functions; `reload()` honors `proxy.lock`.
